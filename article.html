Start with this

A useful bedroom plan shows more than a bed. Mark the door swing, windows and storage first, then compare furniture arrangements against those fixed features.

A room described as “10 × 10” may still have an awkward doorway, a projecting closet or a radiator where you hoped to put a desk. The drawing needs to reflect the room you actually have.

This sample demonstrates our article format. The final guide will use new illustrative layouts checked by Shabaz, with furniture dimensions and assumptions shown alongside each option.

Make the dimensions explicit.

These are the dimensions used to explain the drawing. A bed frame can be larger than its mattress; measure the actual furniture before choosing an arrangement.

Illustrated room interior10′ × 10′
Illustrated mattress footprint60″ × 80″

The mattress footprint is an explicit drawing assumption, not a universal bed-size claim. The final layouts must account for the actual frame, doors, furniture and access needs.

Look at what each choice costs.

  • Access: can the people using the room reach the bed and storage comfortably?
  • Storage: show the open drawers and doors, as well as the closed furniture.
  • Daily routine: decide whether a larger bed, a desk or more storage matters most.

The final comparison will explain these trade-offs with dimensioned drawings. Decorative styling should support a workable arrangement.
